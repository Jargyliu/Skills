# Reference style analysis

Measured from 8 gaming-setup TikToks the user chose as examples (all 576×1024, 30 fps, 11–27 s).
Read this when making a judgment call the script doesn't cover (unusual footage, adding text, choosing a look).

## Two flavors

| | Aesthetic tour (5 of 8) | Fast hype (3 of 8) |
|---|---|---|
| Shot length | ~2 s (4 beats) | 0.7–1.2 s (1–2 beats) |
| Cut timing | on the beat, 100% in four of these | on musical hits (drums/accents) more than the steady beat |
| Camera | slow, smooth slides and push-ins on details | handheld, whip pans, hands in frame (unboxing) |
| Text | none | short hook ("New desk?"), "Link in bio", "Sound test" |

The user said they like **"kinda fast" cuts**, so the default is between the two: 2-beat shots with 1-beat bursts on hard hits (`--pace fast`).

## Shared structure

- **Hook**: opens on a wide or striking view of the whole setup; the first cut comes 1.5–3.5 s in.
- **Middle**: detail shots (monitor, keyboard, mouse, PC case, lighting, desk accessories), each with slow camera movement; nothing is fully static.
- **Ending**: closes on a wide shot matching the opening, so the video loops.
- **Length**: mostly 15–20 s. Vertical 9:16. Hard cuts; one example used a single whip-blur transition.
- **Color**: one consistent look per video. Seen: clean/bright (white desks, daylight), dark/moody (black setups, RGB), faded/desaturated, near black-and-white.

## Jump cuts on the beat (teeqzy videos: examples 4 and 6)

Besides cutting to a new clip, these jump ahead *within* a shot: same camera shot, but the footage skips forward a fraction of a second, landing on a beat. Example 4 does it 1–2 times in most shots (e.g. a keyboard being turned over skips to the next angle on the beat); example 6 does it once early on. Measured, the jumps land on a beat or a drum hit, like the full cuts. They work best on footage with visible motion (hands, pans), where the skip reads clearly. The user explicitly asked for this, at about a quarter second per jump.

## Text, when used

Short (2–3 words), white bold with dark outline, upper third of the frame, only on the hook or the last shot. Never captions over the whole video.
